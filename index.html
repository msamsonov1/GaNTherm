Microscopic Physics of Thermal Trapping & Dynamic $R_{\text{DS(on)}}$ Current Collapse

Gallium Nitride (GaN) high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs) rely on a spontaneous and piezoelectric two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) formed at the AlGaN/GaN heterointerface. Under rapid orbital thermal cycles and high-frequency switching, deep-level energy traps populate and freeze out electrons, creating virtual gates that severely strangle channel conductance ($G$).

1. Trap Emission Time Constant & Freeze-Out

Deep electron traps (predominantly Carbon $C_{\text{N}}$ acceptor states and Iron $Fe_{\text{Ga}}$ substitutionals) exhibit thermally activated Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) emission. As junction temperature $T$ drops during low-power orbital cruising, emission rates exponentially freeze out:

$$\tau_{\text{e}}(T) = \frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{n}} v_{\text{th}} N_{\text{c}}} \exp\left(\frac{E_{\text{C}} - E_{\text{T}}}{k_{\text{B}} T}\right)$$

Where $\sigma_{\text{n}}$ is electron capture cross section ($\sim 10^{-15}\text{ cm}^2$), $v_{\text{th}} = \sqrt{3 k_{\text{B}} T / m^*}$ is the electron thermal velocity, $N_{\text{c}}$ is conduction band effective density of states, and $(E_{\text{C}} - E_{\text{T}}) \approx 0.85\text{–}0.95\text{ eV}$ represents the deep trap activation barrier.

Cryogenic Freeze: At -55°C, $\tau_{\text{e}} > 10^4\text{ s}$, trapping electrons permanently in the off-state.

2. Virtual Gate 2DEG Sheet Carrier Depletion

Electrons trapped in the buffer/surface behave as a negative space-charge layer ("virtual gate"). When switching ON, channel conductance $G$ drops relative to static datasheet values $G_0(T)$ by the un-emitted trapped areal charge density $n_{\text{trap}}(t)$:

$$G(T, t) = \frac{W}{L} q \mu_{\text{2DEG}}(T) \left[ n_{\text{s0}}(T) - \int_0^t \frac{\mathrm{d}n_{\text{trap}}}{\mathrm{d}t} \mathrm{d}t \right]$$

Here $n_{\text{s0}}$ is the equilibrium 2DEG density ($\approx 10^{13}\text{ cm}^{-2}$), $\mu_{\text{2DEG}}(T) \propto T^{-1.5}$ is acoustic phonon-limited mobility, and the dynamic resistance degradation factor is:

$$\Delta R_{\text{DS(on)}}(t) = \frac{1}{G(T,t)} - R_{\text{static}}(T)$$
Result: Dynamic on-resistance expands by up to 2.4x under fast switching duty cycles.

3. State Observer Inversion Algorithm

The gate driver samples voltage drop $V_{\text{DS,on}}$ at the nano-second threshold following turn-on, calculating instantaneous conductance $G_{\text{meas}} = I_{\text{D}} / V_{\text{DS,on}}$. The observer computes the trap deficit state vector $\hat{X}(k)$:

$$\hat{\epsilon}_{\text{trap}}(k) = 1 - \frac{G_{\text{meas}}(T_k, I_k)}{G_{\text{datasheet}}(T_k, I_k)}$$

If $\hat{\epsilon}_{\text{trap}} > \epsilon_{\text{threshold}}$ (typically $12\%$), the Kalman observer flags active current collapse, calculating the required sub-threshold de-trapping field excitation $\mathbf{E}_{\text{stim}}$ to force tunnel emission.

Sampling rate: 50 MSPS on-chip Kelvin source sensing via high-voltage sense FET pad.

4. Field-Assisted Tunneling De-Trapping (Poole-Frenkel)

Rather than waiting seconds for thermal emission, GaNTherm injects microsecond sub-threshold resonant pulses ($V_{\text{GS}} \in [-0.5\text{V}, +1.0\text{V}]$ below $V_{\text{th}} \approx +1.8\text{V}$) during inverter dead-times ($\Delta t_{\text{dead}}$), lowering the barrier through the Poole-Frenkel electric-field effect:

$$e_{\text{n}}(E, T) = e_{\text{n0}} \exp\left[ \frac{q \sqrt{q \mathbf{E}_{\text{local}} / (\pi \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{\text{r}})}}{k_{\text{B}} T} \right]$$

By boosting local electric field $\mathbf{E}_{\text{local}}$ inside the access regions right before conduction, trapped carriers are swept cleanly into the drain before inverter power flow starts.

Efficiency: Recovers 94.6% of lost channel conductance without causing shoot-through.
